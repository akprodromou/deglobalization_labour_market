# Month 2, Week 4: Data-Quality Policy

Every profile in the working sample is tagged with a `data_completeness` label (Full / Inferred / Weak / Sparse) produced by the Tier 1–4 fallback chain (`assign_data_completeness_tier.py`), and this tag determines which analyses a given profile is eligible for. Applied to the 7,222-profile Month 2 sample, the distribution is 62.7% Full (direct skills), 3.1% Inferred (ESCO occupation lookup, via `occupation_uris` for Revelio or free-text exact-match for other sources), 0.0% Weak (sector/degree prior — see below), and 34.2% Sparse. **Skill-based analyses** (the deglobalization-exposure index, task-content classification, and any analysis that depends on a profile's specific skill set) use **Tiers 1–2 only (Full and Inferred, 65.8% of the sample combined)**, since these are the only tiers grounded in either directly observed or ESCO-validated skill data; Sparse profiles are excluded from these analyses entirely rather than imputed with a weak or arbitrary default. **Geographic and demographic analyses** (regional distribution, source-country composition, sectoral participation where sector tags exist) use **all profiles regardless of tier**, since location, source, and demographic fields are independent of skill completeness and excluding Sparse profiles there would needlessly shrink the regional sample without any corresponding gain in skill-data reliability. Tier 3 is implemented and validated but produced zero assignments on this sample: both inputs it depends on (`sectors`, `skills`) are concentrated in Revelio and are close to mutually exclusive within that source (91.6% vs. 12.4% completeness respectively), leaving too small an overlap population (31 profiles, scattered across 48 sector categories) to clear the 20-profile minimum-bucket-size threshold used to guard against low-confidence inference; this is documented as a structural data-quality limitation rather than a pipeline defect, and Tier 3 remains available for later analyses should a substantially larger, Revelio-weighted pull change this balance.
